====================================================================================================
 1. Delivery age   delta_i = t_arrival_GCS(i) - t_egress_UAV(i)
====================================================================================================
Age of the vehicle state carried by telemetry sample i when it reaches the GCS. Sample i is paired
across the two captures by (system_id, time_boot_ms) or sequence; the egress time is stamped at the
UAV before the uplink, so delta_i includes every queue the sample waited in.

====================================================================================================
 2. Belief vs truth   b_i = || p_GCS[i-1] - p_truth(t_arrival_i) ||
====================================================================================================
At each arrival i, the operator still holds the PREVIOUS sample (the frozen belief); the truth is the
latest SITL egress position with t_egress <= t_arrival_i (where the vehicle actually is right then).
It never looks at the plan, so route geometry cannot enter it. It needs the simulator's truth, so it
is the experiment's ruler, not something a real operator can compute (see section 9 for that).
  code:  freshness_metrics.merge_telemetry_downlink -> belief_vs_truth_m
  file:  telemetry_downlink_summary.json  belief_vs_truth_m;  validated in belief_validation

====================================================================================================
 3. Sample freshness   F_GCS,i = 1{delta_i < tau_fresh},  tau_fresh = 150 ms
====================================================================================================
F = 1 means sample i reached the GCS fresh. A_fresh is the share of fresh deliveries in a window.
tau_fresh sits above the baseline tail (delta p99 ~ tens of ms) and far below the attack regime (seconds).
  baseline  ATTACK window:  2040/ 2040 fresh  ->  A_fresh = 1.0000     whole run: A_fresh = 1.0000
  attack    ATTACK window:     2/ 1208 fresh  ->  A_fresh = 0.0017     whole run: A_fresh = 0.1730

  Note: the baseline has no adversary, so its ATTACK window is the same time span with nothing
  injected (a 'virtual' attack window). It is the like-for-like comparison.

====================================================================================================
 4. Network health   H_net = 1{ OWD_cmd_p99 < tau_owd  AND  avail > tau_avail  AND  linkloss = 0 }
====================================================================================================
What the operator's monitors look at: the COMMAND leg (GCS -> UAV) and loss-of-link declarations.
  tau_owd = 1.0 s (1.0 s at >= 95 %, Honeywell TestReport-263 citing DO-377A)
  tau_avail = 99.9 % (campaign validity gate)
  linkloss = loss-of-link declarations a QGC-style monitor would raise (K8)
  file: run_kpi_report.json -> legs_table.command  (owd_p99_s, delivery_rate_post_warmup,
        criterion_C4.checks.QGC_style_gap_3_5s.loss_declarations)
  baseline  OWD_cmd_p99 = 38.6 ms < 1 s  |  avail = 100.000 % > 99.9 %  |  linkloss = 0  (max heartbeat gap 0.14 s)  ->  H_net = 1
  attack    OWD_cmd_p99 = 39.8 ms < 1 s  |  avail = 99.965 % > 99.9 %  |  linkloss = 0  (max heartbeat gap 0.72 s)  ->  H_net = 1

  => H_net = 1 in BOTH runs: the link looks the same to the operator with and without the attack.

====================================================================================================
 5. Vehicle health   H_veh = 1{ trk_truth_p95 < tau_trk  AND  failsafe = 0  AND  mode = GUIDED }
====================================================================================================
trk_truth(t) = || p_truth(t) - p_plan(t) ||: the SITL egress position against the reference at the
same instant. It is PHYSICAL: the truth is immune to delivery delay, and the join only accepts a truth
sample within max_join_gap_ms (500 ms) of the reference instant.
  code: freshness_metrics.compute_trk_truth     file: trk_truth.json -> tracking_error_truth_m
        run_meta.json -> outcomes.failsafe, outcomes.mode_transitions
  baseline  trk_truth p50/p95/p99 = 7.9 / 9.7 / 50.9 m  join rejected: 0  |  failsafe = False  |  modes [('LAND', 'GUIDED'), ('GUIDED', 'LAND')]  ->  H_veh = 1
  attack    trk_truth p50/p95/p99 = 7.9 / 9.6 / 50.5 m  join rejected: 0  |  failsafe = False  |  modes [('LAND', 'GUIDED'), ('GUIDED', 'LAND')]  ->  H_veh = 1

  Sensitivity to tau_trk (p95, the statistic the predicate uses):
     tau_trk (m)   H_veh baseline   H_veh attack
               5                0              0
              10                1              1
              15                1              1
              20                1              1
              30                1              1
              50                1              1
              60                1              1
    Baseline and attack get the SAME H_veh at every tau, because trk_truth does not move under attack.
    Any tau above the baseline p95 (9.7 m) gives H_veh = 1 in both.

  Why p95 and not p99 -- the historical problem, reproduced:
    trk_truth p99 = 50.9 m at BASELINE and 50.5 m under attack. That tail is NOT
    route curves: it is the start-up transient. When the stream begins, the vehicle is still at its
    hover point after takeoff, far from the first reference point, and the error is large until it
    reaches the route. physics_truth.py separates it (harness rule: error < 10 m held for 2 s):
      baseline start-up 8.9 s = 1.6 % of samples, from 97.1 m  |  after it: p95 9.5, p99 10.1, max 12.3 m
               along-track -7.8 m (trailing by 1.96 s), cross-track p50 0.09 m, p99 5.4 m -> the vehicle is ON the path
      attack   start-up 8.9 s = 1.6 % of samples, from 97.1 m  |  after it: p95 9.5, p99 10.1, max 12.3 m
               along-track -7.8 m (trailing by 1.96 s), cross-track p50 0.09 m, p99 5.4 m -> the vehicle is ON the path
    About 1.6 % of the samples is enough to set the p99 but not the p95. With p99 and a tau_trk sized
    for the flight (~10 m), H_veh = 0 in both runs, so SSS_i = 0 everywhere and the predicate would
    never fire -- for a reason unrelated to the attack. p95 keeps the physical reading and leaves the
    start-up transient out.

====================================================================================================
 6. The predicate   SSS_i = H_net * H_veh * (1 - F_GCS,i)
====================================================================================================
H_net and H_veh are WINDOW indicators; only F_GCS,i varies per delivery. When the window holds
H_net = H_veh = 1 the predicate reduces to  SSS_i = 1 - F_GCS,i : the network and the vehicle look
healthy and the sample at the GCS is stale. That is the false-healthy condition, made testable.
  baseline  H_net*H_veh = 1   ATTACK window: 0/2040 deliveries with SSS_i = 1   -> share 0.0000   (1 - A_fresh = 0.0000)
  attack    H_net*H_veh = 1   ATTACK window: 1206/1208 deliveries with SSS_i = 1   -> share 0.9983   (1 - A_fresh = 0.9983)

  => baseline: the gates are open but every sample is fresh, so SSS_i = 0.
     attack:   the gates are open AND almost every sample is stale, so SSS_i = 1. When H = 1 the share
     of SSS_i = 1 is exactly 1 - A_fresh.

  Closing evidence (not part of the formula, but what makes it 'silent'): operator alerts.
    baseline  STATUSTEXT with severity <= 4 inside the ATTACK window: 0
    attack    STATUSTEXT with severity <= 4 inside the ATTACK window: 0

====================================================================================================
 7. Effect criterion C1   effect = 1{ OWD_p50 >= 1 s  OR  OWD_p99 >= 150 ms }  (per leg)
====================================================================================================
Pre-registered before the campaign. Evaluated on each leg separately; 'tail only' when p99 crosses
but p50 stays below 100 ms. file: run_kpi_report.json -> legs_table.<leg>.criterion_C1
  baseline command   p50       20.1 ms  p99       38.6 ms  -> effect False   (pipeline: False)
  baseline return    p50       18.1 ms  p99       30.1 ms  -> effect False   (pipeline: False)
  attack   command   p50       20.2 ms  p99       39.8 ms  -> effect False   (pipeline: False)
  attack   return    p50    13092.1 ms  p99    13779.6 ms  -> effect True   (pipeline: True)

  => under attack the RETURN leg has the effect and the COMMAND leg does not. That pair is the
     false-healthy condition in two lines: the leg the operator monitors stays green.

====================================================================================================
 8. Queueing model   AoI = (Q / C) * (N + 1)
====================================================================================================
With N backlogged co-tenants, proportional-fair gives the victim a share C/(N+1) of the uplink. If the
victim offers more than that share, its RLC buffer fills; the age of a sample at the head is buffer
over drain rate, Q / (C/(N+1)). Q = RLC_TX_MAXSIZE = 10 MB = 80 Mbit; C = cell uplink capacity.
Valid only while demand > C/(N+1); below that there is no backlog and the age stays at baseline.

  Least squares over the N with a backlog:  AoI = 2.565 * (N+1) + 0.193 s
  slope = Q / C   ->   C = 80 / 2.565 = 31.2 Mb/s

      N   k   measured      model    error   note
      1   3      0.02s         --       --   share C/(N+1) = 15.6 Mb/s > 12 Mb/s offered: no backlog, as the model says
      2   3      7.88s      7.69s     2.5%
      3   3     10.46s     10.26s     2.0%
      4   5     13.01s     12.82s     1.5%

  Independent check: the capacity probe (no adversary, victim video raised until it
  self-congests) brackets C(106 PRB) in [24, 32] Mb/s. The fitted C = 31.2 Mb/s is INSIDE it.

====================================================================================================
 9. Destination-side detector   A_i = t_i - (T0_hat + boot_i),  alarm when A_i > 1 s
====================================================================================================
Needs nothing but what the telemetry already carries: boot_i = time_boot_ms stamped at the source
before the uplink, and t_i = the local arrival time at the GCS.
    implied_i = t_i - boot_i          = T0 + D_i      (D_i = transport delay >= 0)
    T0_hat    = running min(implied)  ~ T0            (the fastest sample, D ~ 0, calibrates the epoch)
    A_i       = t_i - (T0_hat + boot_i) = D_i - D_min
No simulator truth is used, so unlike belief_vs_truth this runs on a real ground station (shipped as
the QGroundControl detector build, --open-qgc-mitigation).

  baseline  ATTACK window: detector A p50 = 84.6 ms   oracle delta p50 = 18.1 ms
            samples above 1 s: 0/2040

  attack    ATTACK window: detector A p50 = 13159.8 ms   oracle delta p50 = 13092.1 ms
            samples above 1 s: 1203/1208   first alarm at t_rel 192.6 s, attack onset 190.2 s (+2.4 s)

  Why the detector does not equal the oracle exactly -- clock drift:
    On the baseline run, where D_i is ~constant, implied_i drifts at 0.2627 ms/s (263 ppm): the autopilot's
    boot clock and the GCS clock do not tick at exactly the same rate (in SITL, simulated time runs
    slightly behind wall time). The running minimum is captured early and never moves again, so A_i
    grows by drift x (time since calibration). That is the tens-of-ms gap to the oracle above --
    irrelevant against a 1 s threshold and a 13 s effect, but it does not stay irrelevant forever:
    at this rate a fresh link would cross the 1 s threshold after 3806 s (~63 min) of mission,
    i.e. a FALSE alarm on a long flight. A deployed detector needs a leaky minimum (or a drift
    estimate from a known-good phase) rather than a minimum held for the whole mission.

  Second limitation, reproduced -- calibration needs a FRESH sample:
    If the ground station only connected once the backlog was already established, every D_i it ever
    sees is already ~13 s, T0_hat absorbs that delay, and the detector reads only the variation around
    it. Calibrating only from the first sample whose delay already reached steady state (delta > 11.8 s)
    gives A p50 = 1146.0 ms and 855/1160 alarms,
    instead of 13159.8 ms and 1203/1208. In the campaign the GCS connects before the attack, so
    calibration is clean; a deployment must calibrate during a known-good phase.

====================================================================================================
 10. Traps -- numbers that look right and are not
====================================================================================================
  (a) 'Tracking error' computed from the BELIEF is not physical.
      The testbed also reports cross_track / max_deviation (run_meta.json -> outcomes). They are
      computed from p_GCS, the position as received (setpoint_stream.py: cur = poll.ned_cache[0]),
      against the plan now -- so they restate delta_i in meters (~ speed x age).
                   cross_track p99 (belief)   trk_truth p95 (truth)   trk_truth p99 (truth)
      baseline                        5.4 m                   9.7 m                  50.9 m
      attack                         50.4 m                   9.6 m                  50.5 m
      The belief-derived column jumps under attack; the truth columns do not move. The airframe flies
      its path. Never use cross_track or max_deviation as evidence of physical deviation, and never
      put them in H_veh: they rise exactly when the attack works, which would switch the predicate off.

  (b) Whole-run belief understates the attack.
      attack run: whole run 38.82 m   vs   ATTACK window 47.82 m
      The pipeline's per-run belief covers the whole measurement window, which includes fresh
      pre-attack and drain samples. Both numbers are right; quote the window with the number.

  (c) Two t_rel origins, about 90 s apart.
      flyblind_phase_windows.json, event_markers.csv and clock_anchor.json count from T0 (run start).
      The MAVLink captures' own t_rel_s column (telemetry_downlink_gcs.csv, statustext_gcs.csv, and
      c2.json times) counts from the mission start. Slicing those columns against the phase windows
      shifts every event by the offset. Measure it from a file that carries both clocks:
        offset = (t_mono_ns - t_rel_s * 1e9 - T0_mono_ns) / 1e9
      baseline   offset = 90.277 s
      attack     offset = 90.326 s
      Every script here assigns phases from t_mono_ns against T0_mono_ns, never from that column.
